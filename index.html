Opening Routines

Setting the foundation for a successful operating day

⚠️ Critical Priority

The first thing when opening is to unlock the fire doors so people can exit in case of emergency. You must also ensure you have a green vest available to direct guests in the event of a fire alarm.

Bar Area Setup

  • Count cash and register beers, note the amount counted
  • Fill the register numbers
  • Turn on all machines and check them inside - wash extra inside
  • Boil water for the sinks
  • Take trash buckets to the bar area
  • Check ice buckets (wash) and prepare ice
  • Take the cups from the bottles and wash them in the machine
  • Clean the sinks with boiling water
  • Get ice ready before guests arrive

Floor & Guest Area

  • Check for bookings, write them out and place on tables (make cheat sheet if many)
  • Walk around and check that pool cues are in their proper place
  • Check for glasses under artwork/decorations/pool tables
  • Check all sitting areas for spilled items or mess
  • Verify everything is in place from yesterday
  • Ensure all tables are clean and washed
  • Take chairs down from tables
  • Place pillows in designated areas
  • Put cues, chairs, and triangles in their proper places
  • Prepare candles (ready to light at 17:00)
  • Light up dart areas
  • Turn on lights

Final Opening Steps

  • Set correct lighting throughout the venue
  • Turn on music in main areas
  • Turn on music in the Blackroom
  • Check Blackroom and Redroom are tidy and ready - notify managers via chat
  • Double check if the fire doors are open
  • Go downstairs and unlock entrance door for guests

After Opening the Door

These tasks can be completed after guests arrive, as you have time between service:

  • Set up coffee machine
  • Check and restock all fridges
  • Supply walk-in fridges as needed (bartender 2/3?)
  • Check wine cabinet - clean it and remove fingerprints
  • Check storage areas if tidy, plan tidying up if not
  • Go through juice cabinet - discard/store as needed
  • Check dates on all syrups that will go bad within a few days
  • Pour syrups into new bottles if needed
NOTE: Monin syrup containers become disgusting after a long day - they need to be cleaned more often. At minimum, rinse thoroughly in hot water! No nasty bottles in da house.

During Shift Routines

Continuous service and maintenance throughout your shift

Continuous Guest Service

  • Clean billiard tables after guests use them (use holy water)
  • Clean billiard balls after they are used
  • Clean and wipe tables after guests leave
  • Wipe down the bar counter regularly
  • Regularly go outside to collect dirty glasses
  • Monitor guests and control alcohol levels
  • Sweep/clean the bar area - watch for beer stains and spills
  • Put cues and triangles back in their proper places
  • Continuously restock fridges and supplies as needed
  • Refill paper in the toilets as needed
  • Check toilets regularly, remove paper from the floor
  • Re-light candles as needed

At 17:00 (5 PM)

  • Change lighting throughout the venue
  • Light all candles

Bar Maintenance During Shift

  • Restock liquor bottles as they run low
  • Restock fridges and pool room supplies
  • Put clean and cold glasses in their proper places
  • Keep an eye on which glasses are needed, notify management if needed
  • Polish pool balls regularly
  • Get ice as needed
  • Empty and close dishwashers when full
  • Help with floor cleaning if necessary, tidy at all times
  • Take pant & kegs downstairs when needed
  • Change beer kegs when needed
  • Pant bag from walk-in fridge needs to be taken downstairs with the pants
Note: Watch the floor constantly - you can often spot drunk guests, broken glasses, spills, or inappropriate items before security does. Stay vigilant for anything that doesn't belong at Cue.

🧘 Meditation & Breathwork

Quick techniques to reduce stress, stay calm, and reset during shifts

🌟 Why This Matters

Working in hospitality can be intense. These quick techniques help you stay centered, reduce stress, and maintain energy throughout your shift. Even 30 seconds can make a difference!

⚡ Quick Breathing Techniques (30 seconds - 2 minutes)

📦 Box Breathing (2 minutes)

Perfect for stressful moments. Used by Navy SEALs for staying calm under pressure.

  1. Inhale through nose for 4 counts
  2. Hold for 4 counts
  3. Exhale through mouth for 4 counts
  4. Hold empty for 4 counts
  5. Repeat 4-6 times

✨ Best for: Anxiety, overwhelm, before difficult conversations

⚡ 4-7-8 Breathing (1 minute)

Instantly calms the nervous system. Great for quick resets.

  1. Exhale completely through mouth (whoosh sound)
  2. Inhale quietly through nose for 4 counts
  3. Hold for 7 counts
  4. Exhale through mouth for 8 counts (whoosh)
  5. Repeat 3-4 times

✨ Best for: Quick stress relief, resetting between rushes, before break

🔋 Energizing Breath (30 seconds)

Quick energy boost when you're feeling tired or sluggish.

  1. Stand up straight, shoulders back
  2. Quick inhale through nose (1 count)
  3. Quick exhale through mouth (1 count)
  4. Repeat rapidly for 20-30 breaths
  5. Finish with one deep breath in, hold 5 seconds, slow exhale

✨ Best for: Mid-shift energy dip, after cleaning tasks, before busy period

🌊 Grounding Techniques (1-3 minutes)

👁️ 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding (2 minutes)

Brings you back to the present moment when feeling overwhelmed.

  1. 5 things you can SEE - Look around, name 5 objects
  2. 4 things you can TOUCH - Feel textures around you
  3. 3 things you can HEAR - Notice sounds in the environment
  4. 2 things you can SMELL - Focus on any scents
  5. 1 thing you can TASTE - Notice taste in your mouth

✨ Best for: Feeling scattered, anxiety, after difficult customer interaction

🦶 Body Scan (3 minutes)

Release physical tension that builds up during shifts.

  1. Stand or sit comfortably, close eyes if possible
  2. Start at feet - Notice any tension, wiggle toes
  3. Move to legs - Tense and release muscles
  4. Scan torso - Deep breath, soften belly
  5. Check shoulders - Roll them back, drop tension
  6. Relax face - Unclench jaw, soften forehead
  7. Take 3 deep breaths

✨ Best for: Physical tension, neck/shoulder pain, during breaks

💭 Quick Mental Reset (1-2 minutes)

🎯 One-Minute Mindfulness

Simple awareness practice you can do anywhere.

  1. Set intention: "For one minute, I'm just present"
  2. Focus on your breath - natural rhythm
  3. When thoughts come (they will!), just notice them
  4. Gently return focus to breath
  5. No judgment, no "doing it wrong"

✨ Best for: Racing thoughts, before shift starts, mental clarity

🌟 Positive Reset Phrase

Quick mental reframe when things feel overwhelming.

Repeat 3 times (out loud or in your head):

  • "I can handle this moment"
  • "This feeling is temporary"
  • "I've got through harder things before"
  • "One task at a time"
  • "I choose calm" (simple but powerful)

✨ Best for: Feeling overwhelmed, self-doubt, frustration

⏰ When to Use These Techniques

Before Shift:

One-Minute Mindfulness + Box Breathing = Clear head, ready energy

During Rush:

4-7-8 Breathing (in storage room) = Quick reset between orders

After Difficult Customer:

5-4-3-2-1 Grounding + Positive Reset Phrase = Back to baseline

On Break:

Body Scan = Release physical tension, recharge energy

Feeling Tired:

Energizing Breath = Natural energy boost (better than coffee!)

End of Shift:

4-7-8 Breathing = Let go of work stress, transition home

💡 Pro Tips
  • No space is private? Go to the bathroom for 2 minutes - totally normal!
  • Breathing looks weird? Do it while organizing or cleaning - nobody notices
  • Can't remember steps? Just focus on slow, deep breaths - that's 80% of the benefit
  • Doesn't work immediately? That's normal! Effects build with practice
  • 30 seconds is better than nothing - don't wait for "perfect conditions"

🌟 Remember

Your mental and physical wellbeing directly impacts your ability to serve guests well. Taking 30 seconds to reset isn't selfish - it's professional.

You can't pour from an empty cup. Take care of yourself! 💚

Closing Procedures

Two-person closing checklist

Team Requirement

Closing requires two people: one handles the outside/floor area, the other handles the bar interior. Work together to ensure everything is properly closed and cleaned.

Person 1: Outside & Floor Area

  • Close the door downstairs and bring the pant bag with you
  • Clean all pool tables
  • Clean all guest tables
  • Clean table tennis tables
  • Put locks on the fire exit door
  • Turn off all dart lights
  • Arrange all chairs: some on tables, some neatly on floor
  • Put cues, triangles, and pillows in their proper places
  • Gather all chalk (kritt) and return to storage
  • Pull chairs and stools into their usual positions
  • Put chairs up on stage
  • Turn off music in Blackroom
  • Check for lost and found items - place next to entrance or in the box
  • Lock side door and main door
  • Verify everything has been done properly
  • Turn off lights
  • Turn on alarm
Important

If you want Blackroom and Redroom to be cleaned, leave doors OPEN for cleaning staff!

Person 2: Inside Bar Area

Equipment & Surfaces

  • Clean all cocktail equipment from used stations
  • Clean silver plates from the water station
  • Clean rubber mats (gumms) for beer taps
  • Clean all surfaces with Jif including cash register (kassa), terminals, and water towel holder
  • Put on boiling water and wash all sinks thoroughly
  • Boil water and clean all beer taps with it - use glass method

Bottles & Syrups

  • Close all bottles with caps
  • Check shelves for any bottles that need caps
  • Clean syrup bottles so they're not sticky - use warm water and clean with paper towels
  • Double check wine bottles have dates on them

Glasses & Dishwasher

  • Clean all glasses
  • Wash the tray that holds dirty glasses just before turning off machine
  • Turn off the dishwashing machine
  • Wash the machine and filters properly
  • Dry inside the machine with paper towels to remove all residue
  • Use vinegar to wash the door inside and outside
  • Wipe all machines inside after turning off

Trash & Cleaning

  • Carry all trash bags to the hallway
  • Check staff storage for additional trash
  • Empty pant
  • Take out all trash
  • Get better at cleaning all sinks thoroughly

Cash & Reporting

  • Send out faktura (check tabs) - check "bookingtjenester" for faktura info
  • Take Z-report from all registers
  • Print out X and Z reports
  • Write down the numbers and add comments if needed
  • Fill the register numbers (oppgjør)
  • Count all cash
  • Write the date and amount on plastic bag
  • Take cash to the safe
  • Report any missing pool or dart cards to management

Final Tasks

  • Restock the fridges - Check all fridges and restock from storage

Closing Floor

  • Clean remaining space after guests leave
  • Put up all chairs or gather them together
  • Place table cloths in the storage room in redrum, or in a plastic bag and leave it in the redrum bar

Keep the door into Redroom & Blackroom open after closing, so the washing people know they are supposed to wash there.

Guest Service Excellence

Creating memorable experiences for every guest

The Fundamentals of Great Service

First Impressions

  • Greet guests within 30 seconds of their arrival at the bar
  • Make eye contact and smile genuinely
  • Use welcoming language: "Hi! What can I get started for you?" or "Welcome to Cue!"
  • If busy, acknowledge them: "I'll be right with you!" with a smile

Active Listening

  • Give guests your full attention when taking orders
  • Repeat the order back to confirm: "So that's two beers and a gin & tonic?"
  • Listen for preferences and remember regular customers' favorites
  • Pay attention to body language - hesitation might mean they need help deciding

Positive Communication

  • Use positive language: "I can do that" instead of "That's not possible"
  • Be enthusiastic about our products and venue
  • Avoid negative or complaining talk about work, management, or customers
  • Keep conversations professional but warm and friendly

Anticipating Needs

  • Check on guests after serving: "How is everything?" or "Can I get you anything else?"
  • Notice when drinks are running low and offer refills
  • Bring extra napkins, water, or ice without being asked
  • Be attentive to pool players who might need chalk, cues, or triangle

Upselling Techniques

Helping guests discover better options while increasing revenue

The Golden Rule of Upselling

Never just take the order - engage and offer choices. Our premium products are excellent quality, and guests appreciate knowing their options.

Spirits & Cocktails

Ask, Don't Assume

Instead of automatically pouring house brands, create a conversation:

Guest: "I'll have a gin and tonic."

❌ Wrong: [Silently pours house gin]

✅ Right: "Great choice! What kind of gin would you like? We have [mention 2-3 premium options] or our house gin."

Recommend with Confidence

When guests ask for suggestions, give specific recommendations:

❌ Wrong: "We have a lot of gins, anything is fine."

✅ Right: "I really like [specific gin] - it has a nice botanical flavor that works great in a G&T. Or if you prefer something smoother, [another option] is excellent."

The Premium Cocktail Strategy

  • Present the full cocktail menu, including premium options
  • Describe what makes certain cocktails special or unique
  • Create curiosity: "Have you tried our [signature cocktail]? It's really popular."
Psychology Tip: Having high-end cocktails on the menu makes regular cocktails feel more affordable by comparison.

Beer & Wine

Suggest Upgrades

Guest: "Can I get a beer?"

You: "Of course! We have [mention 2-3 options including a craft/premium option]. What sounds good to you?"

Wine by the Glass vs. Bottle

  • If 2+ people order wine, suggest a bottle: "Would you like to share a bottle? It's better value."
  • Describe wine character: "The red is full-bodied and smooth" instead of just listing names

Food & Snacks

Suggest Food with Drinks

  • When taking drink orders: "Can I get you anything to snack on while you play?"
  • Mention popular items: "Our [specific snack] goes really well with beer."
  • For groups: "Would you like to share some [appetizer]?"

Round Upselling

Timing is Everything

  • Check back when drinks are 3/4 finished: "Ready for another round?"
  • For groups: "Can I get the next round started for everyone?"
  • Suggest trying something different: "Want to try a different beer this time?"

Upselling Best Practices

  • Always be genuine - only recommend products you actually think are good
  • Read the guest - if they seem budget-conscious, don't push expensive items
  • Know your products - taste everything so you can speak authentically
  • Use descriptive language that creates appetite appeal
  • Never make guests feel bad for choosing house/cheaper options
  • Accept "no" gracefully and move on - don't be pushy
  • Make it about enhancing their experience, not just making more money

Handling Difficult Situations

Staying calm, professional, and solution-focused

The LAST Framework

Use this framework for any difficult situation:

  • Listen - Let them speak without interrupting
  • Apologize - Show empathy even if it's not your fault
  • Solve - Offer a concrete solution
  • Thank - Thank them for bringing it to your attention

Common Difficult Scenarios

Scenario 1: Complaint About Wait Time

Guest: "I've been waiting 20 minutes for my drink!"

❌ Wrong: "We're busy, you can see that. You'll have to wait."

✅ Right: "I'm really sorry about the wait - we're slammed tonight. Let me get your drink made right now, and I'll add [small comp like chips] for the inconvenience. What were you ordering?"

Scenario 2: Wrong Order

Guest: "This isn't what I ordered."

❌ Wrong: "That's what you said you wanted."

✅ Right: "I apologize for the mix-up. Let me make you exactly what you wanted right away. What should it be?"

Key Point: Never argue about who's right. Fix it quickly and move on.

Scenario 3: Quality Complaint

Guest: "This beer tastes off."

❌ Wrong: "No one else has complained. It's fine."

✅ Right: "I'm sorry it doesn't taste right to you. Let me get you a fresh one, or would you prefer to try something different? No charge for this one."

Scenario 4: Pricing Dispute

Guest: "This is way more expensive than I thought!"

❌ Wrong: "You should have asked before ordering."

✅ Right: "I understand - that's our premium [product]. I should have mentioned the price when you ordered. For next time, our house option is [lower price] if you'd prefer that."

Prevention: Always mention prices for premium products when taking orders to avoid this situation.

Handling Intoxicated Guests

⚠️ Legal Responsibility

We have a legal duty not to serve intoxicated guests. When in doubt, refuse service. It's better to upset one guest than face legal consequences.

Signs of Intoxication

  • Slurred speech or difficulty speaking
  • Unsteady walking or balance problems
  • Aggressive, loud, or inappropriate behavior
  • Difficulty focusing eyes or maintaining eye contact
  • Spilling drinks or fumbling with objects

How to Refuse Service

  • Be firm but polite: "I'm not able to serve you any more alcohol tonight."
  • Don't argue or debate - state it once clearly and don't back down
  • Offer alternatives: "I can get you water or a soft drink, or help arrange transportation home."
  • Involve management or security immediately if they become confrontational
  • Document the incident - note the time and situation

Phrasing Examples

Polite Refusal: "I care about your safety, and I can't serve you any more alcohol tonight. Can I get you some water?"

Firm Refusal: "I've made my decision and it won't change. I'm happy to get you water or call you a taxi."

When They Argue: "I understand you're upset, but this is final. I need you to either have water or I'll need to ask you to leave."

Pro Tip: If people acting nice but they are drunk, tell them that you need to cut them because you want to keep them here, and our guards need to follow sjenkeloven, so they have water for now and let's see! For the Rooms go to Line right away!

Handling Aggressive or Rude Guests

Stay Calm and Professional

  • Never raise your voice or match their energy
  • Keep your body language neutral and non-threatening
  • Speak slowly and clearly in a calm tone
  • Don't take it personally - they're upset about the situation, not you

De-escalation Techniques

  • Acknowledge their feelings: "I can see you're frustrated."
  • Lower your voice - this often makes them lower theirs too
  • Give them space - don't crowd them
  • Focus on solutions: "Let's figure out how to fix this."
  • If they won't calm down, get management or security immediately

When to Involve Others

Get help immediately if:

  • Guest becomes physically threatening or aggressive
  • Situation is escalating despite your best efforts
  • You feel unsafe or uncomfortable
  • Guest is harassing other customers
  • You've tried the LAST framework and it's not working

Payment Issues

Declined Card

❌ Wrong: "Your card was declined" [said loudly]

✅ Right: [Quietly] "The card reader isn't going through - do you have another card you'd like to try?"

Can't Pay / "I'll Pay Later"

  • Stay calm and professional
  • Explain politely: "I need payment before you leave."
  • Offer solutions: "Can someone you're with cover it? Or we accept mobile payment apps."
  • Get management immediately - don't let them leave
  • Never chase someone who runs - note their description and inform management

General Conflict Resolution Tips

  • Choose your battles - sometimes letting small things go is worth it
  • Never argue in front of other guests - take it to a private area if needed
  • Empower yourself to offer small compensations (free drink, discount) within reason
  • Document serious incidents in writing after they occur
  • Debrief with team after difficult situations to learn and improve
  • Remember: A great recovery can turn an unhappy guest into a loyal one
  • Take care of yourself - difficult situations are stressful, talk about it with teammates
💚 Safety First

ALWAYS put safety first. You are already our hero, so no need to prove it! <3
